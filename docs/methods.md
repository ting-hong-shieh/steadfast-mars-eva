One row for each thing the astronaut sees. "Measured" means it comes from NASA data. "Model"
means a published method applied to that data. "Assumed" means a number we chose. "Simulated"
means it is made up for the demo. A row without a source says so.

## What the numbers say about the present demo

- The default plan (steep ground on, 0 kg, 08:08) is 738 m of grid path, 716 m along the drawn
  line, and takes about 20 minutes of walking with this model. The longest return time from any
  point on it is about 6 minutes (read from `window.__route` in the app on 2026-11-15). The
  length changes when the plan changes.
- With an 8 hour limit the margin never reaches zero on this loop. On the free walk the
  turn-back state only appears when the clock is moved forward by hand. A 600 m square is too
  small to show the turn-back decision by walking alone. The two scenarios and the tour steps
  "margin" and "late plan" move the clock for that reason. The whole plan, with 25 minutes of
  work at each of the three stations, takes about 95 minutes and 157 g of oxygen (plan screen,
  read in the app on 2026-11-15).
- 134 of 1,036 footholds on the loop fail the tests, most of them on slopes above the assumed
  25° limit (counted on 2026-11-14 on an earlier route, not counted again). The path model slows
  down on slopes and on costly ground. Since 2026-11-15 ground steeper than 24.2° is an obstacle
  when the plan says so (row Steep ground): a soft one, crossed only where no way around exists.
  A big rock inside the two classified patches still only costs 1.5 times more oxygen.
- The planner passes 30 checks with a known answer (`cd app && npm test`), among them a ridge
  with a gap (the obstacle rule goes through the gap), a wall and its shadow, and the sun line. One of them
  shows a limit of the method: on an 8-neighbour grid a path that is not along an axis or a
  diagonal comes out up to 8 % too long. In the check, 116.6 m for a straight 107.7 m.
- **What the choices do across the whole square** (`cd app && npm run routes`, result in
  `docs/checks/route_factors_check.json`, 2026-11-15). The way home was planned from 5,141 places,
  one every 8 m, each more than 30 m from the habitat. All of it is model output on the 2 m grid.
  - Steep ground. The straight line home crosses ground steeper than 24.2° from 81.3 % of the
    places, the path of least oxygen from 66.2 %, and the path with the obstacle rule from 21.9 %
    (median 0 m, 90th percentile 2 m, against 5 m and 81 m for least oxygen alone). The rule costs
    a median of 0.3 % more oxygen and, for one place in ten, 24 % or more.
  - Oxygen. The straight line home costs a median of 6.0 % more oxygen than the path of least
    oxygen (upper quartile 12.1 %, 90th percentile 25.6 %), and more than 10 % more from 30.8 % of
    the places. **Limit:** 65.5 % of the straight lines have a step steeper than the grades
    Minetti measured, where the oxygen model is outside its data. For the other 1,773 places the
    median is 2.0 % and the 90th percentile 6.6 %. So most of the oxygen gap comes from steep
    steps, and its size there is not supported by data.
  - The shortest path on the grid costs a median of 1.0 % more oxygen than the path of least
    oxygen, and 10.5 % at the 90th percentile.
  - A grid path comes out up to 8 % longer than a free path, which counts against the planned
    paths in these comparisons.
- **Orbital ground against Curiosity's tilt sensor** (`tools/tilt_check.py`, result in
  `docs/checks/curiosity_tilt_check.json`). At the 61 Curiosity stops inside the square (sols
  4594 to 4855, S9), a plane fitted to the 1 m orbital heights over 3 m (the rover's wheelbase,
  chosen before the first run) is on average 7.2° steeper than the rover's measured tilt. Mean
  absolute difference 8.4° (95 % bootstrap interval 6.7° to 10.2°), median 6.5°, correlation
  0.40. Mean rover tilt 9.3°, mean ground tilt 16.5°. The heights agree: rover elevation minus
  the orbital ground is a median 0.06 m (interquartile range −0.07 to 0.19 m), so the places
  line up. The gap shrinks as the window grows: 6.7° over 5 m, 5.0° over 9 m, 2.7° over 21 m.
  So the 1 m orbital ground is rougher than the ground the rover stood on at the scale of a few
  metres. Our route grid (2 m) and the slope row (2 m) work at that scale, so on orbital ground
  they likely show slopes too steep, and walking times and oxygen too high. Inside the 48 m
  Navcam square the ground is Curiosity's own stereo and this check does not apply. Not
  changed in the app yet.


- **Ground types from orbit: checked, not drawn (2026-11-15).** The camera model gives ground
  types on two 48 m patches only. `tools/build_ground_estimate.py` tests whether six features of
  a 1 m cell (brightness of the 25 cm orthoimage, its spread inside the cell and over 3 m, slope,
  roughness and height against the 15 m mean, each standardized per place) predict the camera
  model's most common class in that cell, with a class-balanced logistic regression. Trained on
  the HAB patch and tested on the STN A patch, agreement is 0.384 against 0.516 for always
  answering the most common class. The other way it is 0.243 against 0.691. Inside the STN A
  patch (west half to east half) it is 0.49 against 0.42. Shifting the labels by up to 4 m does
  not help. Numbers: `docs/research/2026-11-15-ground-estimate-check.json`. Limits: two patches,
  one linear model, six features, and the labels are a model's output, not ground truth. So the
  app draws no estimated ground type outside the two patches, and the route cost there stays
  "assumed rocky".
- **Ground look and rocks outside the measured square (GENERATED, 2026-11-15).** A shader in
  `app/src/terrain.js` changes colour only. It compares the orthoimage's brightness at a spot
  with the median of the place: dark and flat is drawn as dark rippled sand, bright as light
  plates with cracks, steep as rubble. Up to 11,000 small generated rocks stand on ground that
  is not steep, none inside the 48 m square. This is a drawing style and not a ground type. No
  height, route, time or oxygen number reads it. `?ground=plain` switches it off. Since
  2026-11-15 04:14 no generated rock stays within 3 m of the planned route (123 of 11,000 are
  taken away at the main place): a rock that no camera measured must not read as an obstacle
  that the route ignores. This is the agent's answer to the owner's question of 2026-11-15
  ("keep them, and go around them?"), which the team may change. The other way, a route that
  bends around invented rocks, would put made-up data into a number of the display. A route
  planned again on site, and the way home off the planned route, can still pass a generated rock.
- **Descent to a place (IMAGINED, 2026-11-15).** Between the plan screen and the first walking
  step of the guided tour, and after travel on the world map, the view comes down from 240 m above the walker in about 5 s and lands on the
  walker's eyes. No lander is modelled. The ground it passes is the same ground as in the walk.
  `?cine=0` skips it and `?cinek=<0 to 0.89>` holds it for a capture. The owner kept it on
  2026-11-15 as the cut while a place loads.

- **Small fixes of 2026-11-15 that have no row above.** The dose rate has its number when all
  panels are open. `?tour`, `?explain` and `?t` accept only valid values. The station block shows
  our short wording of the image label's reason under the photo.
